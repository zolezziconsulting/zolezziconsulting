01 — Where it breaks

The product is not the problem.

Expansions into smaller markets rarely fail on technology. They fail on distance: nobody local stands behind the solution and the cycle quietly stalls.

Cold email opens no doors

Peruvian enterprise buyers do not answer vendors with no local reference. The conversation starts with someone already in the circuit.

The cycle is long and in person

Committees, risk, legal and procurement. Carrying that process from another time zone and another language burns budget before close.

A subsidiary is disproportionate

Incorporating an entity and hiring a team to validate a hypothesis is an expensive bet on something still unproven.

04 — Partnership models

The model is decided after the assessment, not before.

There are several ways to work together and none of them is right by default. It depends on your product, how it is delivered, and how much you want to commit at an early stage.

Business development

We identify and open opportunities on your behalf, with a defined and reviewable scope. This is the most common starting point.

Commercial representation

We act as your local counterpart in front of the customer, with your brand at the front. This requires a formal agreement.

Referrals

We bring you qualified opportunities and you run the sales process. The lightest way to begin.

Distribution or resale

Where the product and the contractual framework allow it and the local customer needs in-country invoicing.

We do not offer exclusivity up front, and we do not guarantee sales volume, number of prospects or time to close. Anyone promising that before seeing your product is selling an expectation.
